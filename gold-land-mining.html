Protect Our Gold. Protect Our Land. Protect Our People.

The campaign’s proposed priorities, what is documented, what an MP can and cannot do, and how residents can take part.

What is documented

Items below come from news reports and public statements, checked on 9 October 2026. They are not official records, accounts differ in places, and later developments may be missing.

  1. November 2025

    An Environmental Impact Assessment for an underground gold project at Isulu–Bushiangala was reported to have been submitted to NEMA. It was reported to affect parts of Idakho North and Idakho Central, with land acquisition and compensation to follow Kenyan law and international resettlement standards.

    Sources (news reports, not official records): Capital FM, 12 Nov 2025.

  2. November 2025

    Reports estimated that about 337 acres, mostly privately owned, would be needed and that about 800 households could be affected. Local leaders and artisanal miners raised concerns, including about coexistence with artisanal mining.

    Sources (news reports, not official records): The Star, 17 Nov 2025.

  3. 4 December 2025

    A public participation meeting convened at Isulu met protests. Reports describe confrontations, deaths and injuries; figures differ between reports and are not repeated here.

    Sources (news reports, not official records): Business & Human Rights Resource Centre, 12 Dec 2025.

  4. December 2025

    Kakamega County government was reported to have closed an artisanal mining site after a shaft collapse killed three miners.

    Sources (news reports, not official records): Kenya News Agency, 10 Dec 2025.

  5. 10 December 2025

    Government leaders were reported to have committed to comprehensive public engagement of all stakeholders before any mining begins.

    Sources (news reports, not official records): Kenya News Agency, 10 Dec 2025.

  6. 4 March 2026

    A Kenya Gazette notice of this date was reported in connection with a mining licence application, said to be under review by the responsible ministry.

    Sources (news reports, not official records): Nation, 2026.

  7. 2026 (date to be confirmed)

    A Senate committee held a public hearing at Bushiangala. The Mining Principal Secretary was reported to say the company held only a prospecting licence and could not start mining until a Community Development Agreement was signed.

    Sources (news reports, not official records): The Standard, 2026; Nation, 2026.

Under the Mining Act, royalties are reported to be shared 70% to the national government, 20% to the county and 10% to the community where mining occurs.

Sources (news reports, not official records): Kenya News Agency, 10 Dec 2025.

Primary documents are not linked here because they have not yet been located and checked. See sources.

Eight proposed priorities

Proposals for review, not confirmed promises. No incidents, household counts, compensation outcomes or statistics are claimed beyond the sourced timeline.

  1. Protecting community land interests
  2. Meaningful public participation in mining decisions
  3. Fair, transparent and lawful compensation where applicable
  4. Artisanal miner safety and working conditions
  5. Responsible mining and environmental rehabilitation
  6. Protecting water sources and addressing unsafe or abandoned shafts
  7. Lawful livelihood opportunities for mining communities
  8. Transparency and accountability in natural-resource decisions

What an MP can do, and who else must act

A Member of the National Assembly makes laws, oversees state organs and represents constituents. Many decisions on mining, land and the environment belong to other bodies, and the campaign will not promise what it has no power to deliver.

Priorities, an MP’s legislative, oversight and representative role, and required collaboration

Fair, transparent and lawful compensation where applicableAdvocate for clear compensation rules and for information on how valuations are done; point residents to independent legal advice.The acquiring entity and valuers; the National Land Commission where applicable; the courts; the responsible ministry.
Artisanal miner safety and working conditionsPress for safety enforcement and emergency response; support safety funding through the national budget process.Ministry responsible for mining (inspection); county government; miners’ associations; health and rescue services.
Responsible mining and environmental rehabilitationScrutinise environmental monitoring and enforcement; support clear rehabilitation duties in law.NEMA; county environment officers; licence holders.
